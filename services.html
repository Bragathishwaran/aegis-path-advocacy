Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Have questions about special education advocacy? We've answered the most common ones below.

A special education advocate is a knowledgeable professional who helps families navigate the complex special education system. They review IEPs, attend meetings, help parents understand their legal rights under IDEA and Section 504, prepare documents, and work collaboratively with school teams to ensure children receive appropriate, legally compliant educational services. Unlike attorneys, advocates focus on the educational process rather than litigation.

Having an IEP doesn't guarantee your child is receiving everything they need. Many IEPs contain goals that aren't measurable, services that are insufficient, or accommodations that aren't being implemented. A professional review often reveals significant opportunities to strengthen the existing plan and hold the school accountable for proper implementation.

Attorneys are essential when legal action is required—but most IEP disputes can and should be resolved collaboratively. AegisPoint advocates are special education specialists (teachers, psychologists, SLPs) who understand the educational system from the inside. We focus on building productive relationships with school teams, which typically produces better outcomes for children. We're also significantly more affordable than attorneys, with transparent flat-rate pricing.

We work with children across the full spectrum of disabilities recognized under IDEA, including: Autism Spectrum Disorder, ADHD, Specific Learning Disabilities (dyslexia, dyscalculia, dysgraphia), Intellectual Disabilities, Emotional and Behavioral Disorders, Speech and Language Impairments, Traumatic Brain Injury, Visual or Hearing Impairments, Orthopedic Impairments, and Other Health Impairments. We also work with children seeking 504 Plans who may not qualify for special education.

Absolutely. We regularly work with families who are in active disputes with their school districts. Our due process specialist Natalie Wright has extensive experience with mediation, state complaints, and OCR complaints. We can help de-escalate situations while still achieving meaningful outcomes for your child. In cases requiring formal legal action, we maintain a referral network of special education attorneys.

We serve children from birth through age 21 who are eligible for or receiving special education services. This includes early intervention (birth–3), preschool special education (3–5), elementary and secondary students (5–21), and young adults still in school through age 21. We also specialize in transition planning for students approaching graduation who need support planning their post-secondary path.
